Research Focus

My research focuses on structural econometrics, causal inference (point and partial identification), robust inference, and applied microeconometrics, with applications in health, population, and development economics. I am particularly interested in policy evaluation and the theoretical foundations of econometric identification.

Working Papers

  • The Causal and Heterogeneous Effects of Antenatal Care on Child Immunization: Evidence from Senegal
    Abstract

    This paper studies the causal impact of antenatal care (ANC) adherence on child immunization outcomes in Senegal. Exploiting exogenous variation in access to prenatal health services and accounting for endogenous selection into ANC, I estimate both average and heterogeneous treatment effects. The results show that ANC significantly increases the probability of full immunization, with substantial heterogeneity across socioeconomic groups. These findings highlight the importance of targeted maternal health policies to reduce inequalities in child health investments.

  • Help Me! If You Help Him, Are You Really Helping Him? (with Liliane Bonnal and Pascal Favard)
  • The Effect of Informal Care on Formal Care Use and Mental Well-being among Single-living Elderly in Europe: Heterogeneous Evidence from a Two-stage Free IV Approach
    Abstract

    This paper examines the relationship between informal care provision, formal care utilization, and mental well-being among single-living elderly individuals in Europe. Using a two-stage free instrumental variable approach, I address the endogeneity of informal care and identify heterogeneous effects across demographic and health profiles. The findings reveal that informal care may substitute for formal care for some individuals while complementing it for others, with important implications for long-term care and ageing policies.

  • Revisiting the Formal–Informal Care Relationship: Evidence on Heterogeneous Effects among Older Europeans (with Liliane Bonnal and Pascal Favard)
